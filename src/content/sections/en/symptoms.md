MS is called "The Disease with 1000 Faces" and not without reason. Symptoms can be so different from person to person that sometimes two people with MS don't share a single common symptom.

## Why symptoms are so different

The reason is simple: it depends on exactly where in the nervous system the immune system has caused damage.

The nervous system controls everything — movement, vision, sensation, balance, thinking, even the bladder. When the myelin on a particular group of nerves is damaged, that specific function is disrupted. That's why one person might have vision problems while another has trouble walking. A third might only experience fatigue.

It's important to know: you won't experience everything on the list below. Most people have only some of these symptoms, and they can change over time.

## The most common symptoms

**Fatigue** — by far the most common symptom. This isn't ordinary tiredness — it's a deep exhaustion that doesn't go away with rest and that people around you struggle to understand.

**Numbness and tingling** — a feeling of numbness in the hands, legs, or parts of the face, sometimes accompanied by tingling or "pins and needles."

**Vision problems** — blurred vision, pain when moving the eye, or temporary loss of vision in one eye, most commonly caused by optic neuritis.

**Balance and coordination problems** — dizziness, unsteadiness when walking, or a feeling that "the ground is moving."

**Cognitive difficulties** — trouble with concentration, forgetfulness, slow thinking. People often call it "brain fog."

**Pain** — it can take different forms: burning sensations, sharp stabs, muscle aches, or sensitivity to touch.

**Spasticity** — stiffness or tightness in the muscles, most commonly in the legs, which can make movement difficult.

**Bladder problems** — frequent trips to the bathroom, urgency, or difficulty emptying the bladder.

**[Emotional changes](/en/emotions)** — anxiety, depression, or sudden mood swings. These are not "in your head" — they are a real symptom of the disease.

## The invisible symptoms

Many MS symptoms are invisible from the outside. Fatigue, pain, brain fog, emotional changes — all of these are real but invisible to others. This can be incredibly frustrating because people around you might not understand how hard things are when you "look fine."

If you're experiencing any of these symptoms — you're not making it up. It's the disease, not weakness. If symptoms are new or suddenly get worse, read [what a relapse is and how to recognise it](/en/relapse).
